Robust AI in the Wild Lab

RAW Lab · AI Thrust, HKUST (Guangzhou) · from 2027

Vision models are trained on clean, processed images. The world does not supply those. Real cameras produce RAW sensor data under mixed illumination, in low light, through scattering media, at the wrong exposure. RAW Lab studies what it takes for perception systems to stay reliable when the imaging conditions stop cooperating — and how knowledge of the physics of image formation can get us there.

Research Directions

Physics-grounded visual perception

Bringing sensor physics — RAW signals, noise formation, ISP behaviour — into models that were designed for sRGB. Adapting pre-trained backbones to the data cameras actually produce.

3D vision under degradation

Reconstruction and novel view synthesis when the input is dark, noisy, hazy, or captured through water. Benchmarks and methods for geometry that survives real imaging conditions.

Computational photography

Illumination estimation, white balance, exposure correction, and colour science — with an interest in what vision-language models can contribute to problems traditionally solved by statistics alone.

Embodied and Physical AI

Perception for robots that operate outside controlled environments, where the gap between simulated observations and real sensor data determines whether a policy transfers.

Openings

RAW Lab is recruiting its first cohort. I am looking for people who are curious about how images come to exist, comfortable reading a paper and a datasheet in the same afternoon, and willing to spend time on problems that do not have clean benchmarks yet.
